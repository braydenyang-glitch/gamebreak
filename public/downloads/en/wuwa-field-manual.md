# GameBreak — Wuthering Waves

Wuthering Waves is an open-world action RPG on Solaris-3. You play as Rover and travel with Resonators whose skills, timing and swaps shape combat. Exploration supplies materials and Echoes; focused character and weapon upgrades turn those discoveries into useful progress.

## Learn a Resonator’s actual combat rhythm

Practice a small sequence before copying a long combo.

Manual combat practice; follow the current Resonator’s skill text.

1. Find Skill Training through the Guidebook’s Path of Growth. Read the current Resonator’s Resonance Skill, Resonance Liberation and Forte Circuit before practicing.
2. Practice a basic attack sequence, then a defensive response. Watch the enemy’s animation and the game’s dodge or parry prompts rather than pressing every button at once.
3. Watch your Forte gauge and read what fills or spends it. Use the official guide’s sequence for that specific Resonator; different characters do not share one universal rotation.
4. Check Concerto readiness before swapping for Intro and Outro effects. Compare one short practice run with and without the intended swap, then adjust only one step.

Enemy attacks and character kits have exceptions. This practice routine is not a tested maximum-damage combo.

## Connect teammates, weapons and Echoes

Use character-specific guidance and a repeatable comparison.

Team planning and Echo presets; no current meta ranking.

1. Choose the character whose playstyle you want to build around. Add teammates that support that plan, with recovery or protection if survival is the main problem.
2. Open Guide Depot from the Terminal, or use Kuro’s guide portal. Select the exact Resonator and inspect its weapon, Echo, upgrade-priority and teammate advice.
3. Read the actual Outro effect and the intended recipient before planning a swap. Also check whether your equipped weapon and Echo effects help the role you assigned.
4. Save the current Echo setup as a preset before changing it. Compare the displayed attributes, then test the same encounter so a new build is not judged on a completely different fight.

Guide Depot can contain different guide authors and update times. A recommended set is guidance, not proof that it is best for your account.

## Make a useful material route

Connect upgrade screens, Collection Spots and reward costs.

Resource planning; no promised drop quantities or active bonus.

1. Pick one upgrade and write down its missing materials and currency. Separate world-gathered items from challenge rewards before choosing a route.
2. For supported gathering materials, open the item from the Backpack or Resonator screen and choose Collection Spots. Track a nearby group rather than wandering without a target.
3. Before a reward claim, inspect the displayed Waveplate cost and reward type. Spend it toward the missing upgrade rather than assuming every activity gives the same materials.
4. Check the inventory after the planned run and stop when the next upgrade is ready. Keep Astrite spending separate from everyday material farming decisions.

A past double-reward event is not a permanent rate. Use the current claim screen and active event notice for actual costs and bonuses.

## Explore a region without losing your route

Use travel points, map layers and unlock information.

General regional exploration; no claim of a complete collectible map.

1. Follow the active main quest until it opens the destination you want. Activate nearby Resonance Beacons so you can return without repeating the whole journey.
2. Open the map’s Region Guide when available. Inspect a feature’s unlock condition instead of guessing why an activity or facility cannot be entered.
3. Check map layers and the tracked objective’s height or entrance. A marker at similar horizontal coordinates can still be in another area or floor.
4. Finish one small route before changing regions: a quest objective, nearby gathering group and return point. Note an inaccessible spot and revisit after its requirement is met.

Not every location is available at the same story stage. Map tools help navigation but do not bypass quest requirements.

## Check a Convene before using your currency

Read the current rules and try the offered character when possible.

Convene literacy; linked event examples are historical, not an active schedule.

1. Open the current Convene and its Details. Check the target, end time, ticket type and conversion cost on the confirmation screen.
2. Read that specific banner’s guarantee and carry-over rules. Character, weapon, beginner or special celebration banners may not follow identical rules.
3. If the current event offers a trial such as First Resonance, try the featured Resonator’s attacks and movement. Decide whether you enjoy the playstyle before deciding to pull.
4. Set a stopping budget and record what you spent. Save for your chosen priority; a historical announcement is a reference for past rules, not proof that its banner is still available.

No result is promised here and no real-money purchase is recommended. The current in-game Details are the authority for your selected Convene.

## Turn exploration into steady account progress

Keep the story moving while preparing a usable core team.

Newcomer progression principles; no claimed current level cap or optimal endgame build.

1. Read the next main quest’s prerequisites and use the Guidebook to choose an available task. A new region’s introduction does not mean every account can enter it immediately.
2. Prioritize a small core of Resonators and their weapons. Check the exact missing upgrade in-game instead of spreading materials across every new character at once.
3. Review the Data Bank and Echo screens as you collect. Distinguish collection progress from the upgrade level and equipment effects of an individual Echo.
4. When combat becomes harder, return to Skill Training and inspect a current character guide. Choose whether the next improvement should be practice, weapon growth, skills or Echo setup.

Guide recommendations and account unlocks can change. Read the current panel before following an older progression checklist or assuming a maximum level.
